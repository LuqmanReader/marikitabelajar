Quant Academy

Open each lesson to understand the mathematics behind your results.

01 — What is quantitative finance?

Quantitative finance uses mathematics, programming and data to investigate pricing, risk and market behaviour. It does not eliminate uncertainty.

02 — Returns and logarithms

For consecutive prices P(t) and P(t−1), the log return is ln(P(t)/P(t−1)). Log returns are convenient because they add across time periods.

03 — Volatility

Volatility is the standard deviation of returns. With daily observations, annualised volatility is approximately daily standard deviation multiplied by √252.

04 — Forecast probability

Our starter model assumes future log returns are normally distributed. Using estimated drift and volatility, it calculates the probability of a future price below 90% of today's level.

05 — Why backtesting matters

Historical fit does not prove predictive skill. Before relying on forecasts, compare them with simple baselines using chronologically separated test periods and evaluate calibration.
